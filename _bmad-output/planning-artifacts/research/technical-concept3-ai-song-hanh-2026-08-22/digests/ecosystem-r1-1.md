# Ecosystem Health — R1 Digest
Dimension: vendor durability, legal/regulatory trajectory, cautionary tales, category-level backlash risk for "AI Song Hành" (persistent AI teacher persona / voice-cloning + LLM stack).

## Findings

- **ElevenLabs raised a $500M Series D in Feb 2026 led by Sequoia at an $11B valuation (up from ~$3.3B in Jan 2025), with revenue growing to ~$500M ARR from $330M in 2025 — indicating strong near-term financial durability for this specific vendor.** [source: TechCrunch, 2026-02-04, accessed 2026-08-22, confidence: high, class: vendor funding/financials] https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/04/elevenlabs-raises-500m-from-sequioia-at-a-11-billion-valuation/

- **ElevenLabs' own Terms of Service reserve the right to change subscription pricing at any time with only 7 days' notice before a price change takes effect (existing paid period is honored, but renewal pricing can change unilaterally); third-party billing-analysis site SpendHound reports enterprise ElevenLabs pricing up ~3485% YoY and SMB pricing up ~46% YoY among its client sample.** [source: ElevenLabs Terms of Use (primary) + SpendHound, accessed 2026-08-22, confidence: medium (SpendHound sample methodology not disclosed; ToS language is primary and high-confidence), class: pricing stability] https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use ; https://www.spendhound.com/marketplace/elevenlabs-pricing

- **ElevenLabs actively deprecates models/APIs on a rolling basis: e.g. the scribe_v1 model is deprecated with removal scheduled July 9, 2026, and old voice-clone IDs are periodically retired when model infrastructure is updated — confirming that even the market-leading, best-funded vendor does not guarantee long-term API/model stability for integrators.** [source: ElevenLabs official documentation changelog, accessed 2026-08-22, confidence: high, class: vendor deprecation history] https://elevenlabs.io/docs/changelog

- **PlayHT/PlayAI, a leading voice-cloning API vendor (comparable tier to ElevenLabs/Cartesia, ~40,000 customers), was acquihired by Meta in July 2025 and permanently shut down Dec 31, 2025 — its public API reportedly went dark in late July, weeks ahead of the announced shutdown schedule, with no data-export tooling and all customer voice clones/audio deleted. This is a concrete, dated instance of a mid-tier voice-API vendor disappearing under a paying customer base within roughly 5 months of acquisition.** [source: TechCrunch (acquisition, primary), 2025-07-13; corroborated by menabytes.com and multiple "PlayHT alternative" migration-guide sites (shutdown timeline/customer-impact detail — lower-tier sources, but consistent across several independent competitor sites), accessed 2026-08-22, confidence: medium-high for acquisition+shutdown fact, medium for granular customer-impact claims (no-refund/no-export details sourced only from competitor marketing pages, not verified against a neutral outlet or PlayHT's own statement], class: vendor shutdown / cautionary tale] https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/13/meta-acquires-voice-startup-play-ai/ ; https://www.menabytes.com/meta-playai/

- **Cartesia (a leading real-time voice-AI/TTS vendor) has raised a $27M seed (Dec 2024, Index Ventures), a $64M Series A (Mar 2025, Kleiner Perkins), and a further $100M round with Sonic-3 model launch — backed by Kleiner Perkins, Index, Lightspeed, NVIDIA — indicating a well-capitalized independent alternative to ElevenLabs, not yet acquired.** [source: Cartesia company blog + Tracxn/Crunchbase aggregation, accessed 2026-08-22, confidence: medium (funding-round facts are commonly reliable via Crunchbase-class aggregators, but not independently cross-checked against a press outlet this round), class: vendor funding] https://www.cartesia.ai/blog/series-a

- **The FTC formally launched a Section 6(b) inquiry on Sept 11, 2025 into AI chatbot "companions," issuing information requests to seven major companies (Alphabet, Character Technologies, Instagram, Meta, OpenAI, Snap, X.AI), focused on emotional/psychological impact on minors, monetization of emotional engagement, and COPPA compliance. This is an active, ongoing federal regulatory inquiry as of the last ~11 months, not a resolved/closed matter.** [source: FTC.gov official press release (primary), 2025-09-11, accessed 2026-08-22, confidence: high, class: regulatory action — open/active] https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-launches-inquiry-ai-chatbots-acting-companions

- **Google and Character.AI agreed in early January 2026 to settle/mediate multiple wrongful-death lawsuits alleging their AI chatbot personas contributed to teen suicides (including a 13-year-old, Juliana Peralta, Sept 2025, and the earlier Sewell Setzer Jr. case). Separately and consequently, Character.AI announced in Oct 2025 it would remove open-ended chat for under-18 users, effective late Nov 2025, as a safety measure — i.e., a leading AI-companion vendor has already had to retreat from serving minors with persona-based conversational AI due to legal/safety pressure.** [source: CNBC, 2026-01-07, and Fortune, 2026-01-08 (two independent outlets); K-12 Dive, accessed 2026-08-22, confidence: high, class: litigation outcome + category-level product retreat] https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/07/google-characterai-to-settle-suits-involving-suicides-ai-chatbots.html ; https://fortune.com/2026/01/08/google-character-ai-settle-lawsuits-teenage-child-suicides-chatbots/

- **The federal NO FAKES Act (establishing a national IP right over one's voice/likeness against unauthorized AI cloning) unanimously advanced out of the Senate Judiciary Committee in June 2026 and is awaiting a full Senate floor vote as of August 2026 — i.e., voice-cloning-specific federal legislation is live and moving, not hypothetical, though not yet enacted. It has support from RIAA/SAG-AFTRA and some digital-rights-group opposition over speech-chilling concerns.** [source: MusicTimes, 2026-06-18 (event date), corroborated by Congress.gov primary bill text (H.R.2794, 119th Congress), accessed 2026-08-22, confidence: medium-high (bill status confirmed via Congress.gov primary source; MusicTimes is a trade-press secondary source for the committee-vote date), class: regulatory trajectory — pending legislation] https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/2794/text ; https://www.musictimes.com/articles/112158/20260618/no-fakes-act-advances-senate-committee-vote-moving-forward-ai-voice-cloning-protections-music.htm

## Leads (worth a follow-up round)

- **PlayHT shutdown granular customer-impact claims (no data export, mid-cycle billing without refund) are sourced only from "PlayHT alternative" migration/marketing pages competing for its former customers — these have an incentive to dramatize the shutdown.** Worth verifying against a neutral outlet, Reddit/G2 threads directly, or an archived PlayHT customer-communication/status page before treating the granular claims (not just the shutdown-date fact) as settled.
- **No direct evidence this round on Google/Azure/OpenAI audio API pricing-stability or deprecation history specifically** — only ElevenLabs was checked in depth. A dedicated round on OpenAI Realtime/Audio API and Azure/Google neural-voice deprecation policies would round out the vendor comparison the decision brief asks for ("ElevenLabs, PlayHT, Cartesia, OpenAI audio, Google/Azure").
- **Contradiction/tension to flag for the decision:** ElevenLabs looks financially strong (est. $11B valuation, high revenue growth) yet still (a) reserves unilateral pricing-change rights with only 7-day notice and (b) actively deprecates models/clone-IDs — durability of the company does not equal API/contract stability for a downstream integrator. This is the core "regret risk" tension worth surfacing explicitly in the decision memo.
- **Category-level minor-safety backlash (FTC 6(b) inquiry, Character.AI/Google lawsuits and settlement, Character.AI's own retreat from under-18 open-ended chat) is squarely about persona-based conversational AI aimed at emotional engagement with minors — directly analogous to "AI Song Hành"'s persistent-persona/continuous-supervision concept for K-12 students.** This is arguably the single highest-severity risk surfaced this round and should be weighted heavily in the decision, independent of which TTS vendor is chosen.
