# Deep-research prompt — Chính sách & quy mô thị trường AI-edtech Trung Quốc

> Dán nguyên văn phần dưới đây (từ "## Prompt" trở xuống) vào ChatGPT Deep Research. Khi có báo cáo, mang về đây để tôi "process" — gộp vào `research.md` của run `market-ai-tutor-landscape-concept5-2026-08-17` như một vòng Deepen mới.

## Prompt

You are a market research analyst producing a decision-grade briefing for a product team at a Vietnamese EdTech company (Edupia Class, an ESL/K-12 subscription app, ~390,000 VND/month) evaluating whether — and how — to build an AI-tutor concept partly inspired by Chinese "AI dual-teacher" / digital-twin-teacher products (e.g., Doushen/豆神, TAL/Xueersi). Prior research already covered company-level teardowns of ~10 Chinese EdTech-AI players (51Talk, VIPKid/Dino AI, Novakid, DaDaABC, Doushen, Youdao, iFlytek, TAL, New Oriental, Baidu Xiaodu, Yuanfudao, ByteDance, FoloToy). **Do not re-research individual company products or pricing — that ground is already covered.** This assignment is the missing macro layer beneath those company profiles: the policy and market-sizing context that determines whether the whole category is expanding, contracting, or restructuring — and whether it can plausibly export beyond China.

### Research questions (answer each with cited, dated evidence — do not skip any)

1. **Market size & growth of the AI-in-education segment in China specifically** (not the broader K-12 tutoring market as a whole): total market value, growth rate/CAGR, and — if available — a breakdown by sub-segment (school/institutional procurement of AI teaching systems vs. consumer-facing B2C AI tutor apps/subscriptions vs. AI learning hardware such as AI-enabled learning tablets/point-readers). Prefer named research firms (iResearch/艾瑞咨询, CIC, Frost & Sullivan, Deloitte/McKinsey China EdTech reports), Chinese government (MOE/教育部) publications, or aggregated public-company disclosures over blog aggregators.
2. **Regulatory trajectory since "双减" (Double Reduction, 2021)**: what is the current (2025–2026) state of enforcement and policy toward after-school tutoring vs. AI-specific education products? Has the government's posture toward AI-in-education tightened, loosened, or bifurcated (i.e., treating AI tools differently from traditional tutoring)? Cite any specific 2024–2026 regulatory documents, MOE circulars, or enforcement actions.
3. **AI-specific regulation affecting EdTech products**: China's Generative AI Measures (生成式人工智能服务管理暂行办法, effective 2023) and any algorithm-filing/registration requirements (算法备案) — how do these apply to AI tutoring products aimed at minors? Any child-data-privacy rules specific to AI EdTech (e.g., under the Personal Information Protection Law) that shape product design (e.g., restrictions on emotion/facial monitoring of children, voice cloning consent requirements)?
4. **National AI+Education strategy and top-down drivers**: government initiatives, pilot programs, or funding explicitly promoting AI adoption in classrooms (e.g., national "AI+Education" plans, provincial pilot deployments, procurement programs for AI teaching systems in public schools). Who is funding this — central government, provincial government, or is it purely commercial?
5. **Investment/funding trends**: venture capital and private equity funding into Chinese AI-EdTech companies/products since 2023 — total volume, notable rounds, which sub-segments (AI tutoring apps, AI learning hardware, AI teacher/digital-twin tools) are attracting capital vs. which are being starved. Any recent IPO or delisting activity relevant to this sector.
6. **Export/internationalization signal at the policy level**: is there any government encouragement (or restriction) of Chinese AI-EdTech companies expanding overseas (e.g., "走出去"/going-out policy support, export control relevant to AI models/data), distinct from individual company GTM moves already documented? This should inform whether the sector-wide trend is more or less likely to reach Southeast Asia/Vietnam over the next 12–24 months.

### Evidence standards (non-negotiable)

- **Every claim must carry: publisher, publication date, and (if available) a direct source URL.** No naked numbers — a figure without a named source and date is not usable.
- **Freshness bars**: market size/growth figures must be ≤18 months old; GTM/policy-benchmark claims ≤12 months old. If the only available figure is older, report it explicitly as historical/stale, not as current fact.
- **Two-source rule**: any market-size/growth figure, and any quantitative claim this briefing's recommendation will rest on, needs two independent sources (not two outlets syndicating the same wire story/press release). If only one source exists, say so explicitly rather than presenting it as confirmed.
- **Report contrary evidence.** If sources disagree (e.g., one says the AI-EdTech segment is booming, another says investment has dried up post-regulation), present both sides with their sources — do not average them into a false consensus.
- **Admit gaps rather than padding.** If a research question cannot be answered with credible sources, say so explicitly and explain what was searched.
- Prefer primary/named sources (government publications, named research firms, public-company filings/investor relations) over aggregator blogs or unattributed "industry report" claims.

### Output structure (so it can be processed cleanly)

Structure the report as one section per research question above (six sections), each containing:
- The finding(s), each as a cited sentence (claim + source + date)
- Confidence/caveats for each claim
- Any disagreement between sources found

End with:
- A **full source list** (publisher, title, URL, date, and which finding(s) it supports)
- A short **"gaps and unanswered questions"** section listing anything you could not find credible evidence for
