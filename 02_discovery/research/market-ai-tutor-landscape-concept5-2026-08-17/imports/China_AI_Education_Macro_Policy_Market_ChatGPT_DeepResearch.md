# China AI-in-Education: Macro Market, Policy & Capital Briefing

**Decision-grade briefing for Edupia Class / Vietnam**  
**Research cut-off: 21 August 2026**  
**Scope:** China AI-in-education macro layer only. Individual Chinese company/product/pricing teardowns are intentionally excluded because they were covered in prior research.

---

# Executive Readout

## Bottom line

China's AI-in-education category is **expanding, but it is not a simple continuation of the old EdTech market**. The market is being structurally redirected from regulated, teacher-substitution-oriented K-12 tutoring toward:

- AI-enabled learning
- Adaptive learning
- School/institutional procurement
- Intelligent learning hardware
- AI teacher tools
- Human-AI collaborative education

Three conclusions matter most for Edupia:

1. **Demand is expanding materially.** iResearch's February 2026 study estimates China's 2025 **GenAI+ education product/service market at RMB 344.2bn**, growing at **37% CAGR to RMB 891bn by 2028**. This is a broad GenAI-enabled education market rather than a pure AI-tutor software TAM.

2. **Regulation has bifurcated rather than simply loosened.** Traditional academic after-school tutoring remains heavily constrained and actively monitored in 2026, while the government is simultaneously **explicitly promoting AI+Education** through national policy and pilots.

3. **The Chinese model is exportable at the technology/model level, but not necessarily at the "Chinese tutoring business model" level.** Beijing is actively promoting international digital-education cooperation, while AI, minors and data regulation are becoming stricter.

# 1. China AI-in-Education Market Size & Growth

## Finding 1.1 — iResearch estimates a large and rapidly growing GenAI+Education market

**Finding — iResearch, 10 February 2026:** China's **2025 GenAI+ education product/service market was estimated at RMB 344.2bn**, with a forecast **37% CAGR to RMB 891bn in 2028**.

**Source:** iResearch, *2026 China GenAI+ Education Industry Development Report*, 10 Feb 2026.  
https://report.iresearch.cn/report/202602/4784.shtml

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Caveat:** This is a broad market definition covering education products/services incorporating GenAI. It should **not** be interpreted as the revenue of AI-tutor apps alone.

### iResearch decomposition

| 2025 category | Estimated value |
|---|---:|
| School / institutional procurement | RMB 199.4bn |
| Consumer / C-end education | RMB 144.8bn |
| **Total GenAI+ education products/services** | **RMB 344.2bn** |
| Underlying GenAI technology value | RMB 66.4bn |

**Source:** iResearch, 10 Feb 2026.  
https://report.iresearch.cn/report/202602/4784.shtml

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Important caveat:** iResearch notes that school-side procurement figures are based partly on demand/tender intentions and should not automatically be treated as audited delivered revenue.

---

## Finding 1.2 — Independent research gives a radically smaller market number

**Finding — Grand View Research, updated 27 May 2026:** China's AI-in-education market generated approximately **US$721.9m in 2025**, with a forecast **26.1% CAGR for 2026–2033**, reaching approximately US$5.05bn by 2033.

**Source:** Grand View Research, *China AI in Education Market Outlook*, updated 27 May 2026.  
https://www.grandviewresearch.com/horizon/outlook/ai-in-education-market/china

**Confidence:** Medium-low for direct comparison with iResearch.

### Key disagreement

There is an order-of-magnitude difference:

- iResearch: **RMB 344.2bn (~US$48bn)** in 2025.
- Grand View Research: **US$721.9m** in 2025.

These figures **must not be averaged**.

**Interpretation:** the two sources are measuring different economic layers. iResearch measures the value of education products/services in which GenAI is embedded; Grand View is much closer to the technology/solutions market.

**Decision implication:** for Edupia's business case, use separate layers:

1. AI-enabled education product/service market;
2. underlying AI technology/solution market;
3. pure B2C AI-tutor subscription market.

The third layer could not be isolated credibly from current named research sources.

---

## Finding 1.3 — AI learning hardware is already a material consumer category

**Finding — RUNTO, December 2025:** China's AI learning-tablet market reached an estimated **5.823m units in 2025**, versus 5.068m in 2024. Estimated 2025 sales value was **RMB 17.76bn**, versus RMB 18.76bn in 2024.

**Source:** RUNTO / 洛图科技, *2025 China AI Learning Tablet Market Insight White Paper*, Dec 2025.  
https://pdf.dfcfw.com/pdf/H3_AP202512101797465707_1.pdf?1765365058000.pdf=

**Confidence:** Medium-high for the RUNTO-defined category.

**Interpretation:** unit adoption increased while value declined, suggesting continued demand alongside price/mix pressure.

---

## Finding 1.4 — Smart-learning-machine estimates confirm a tens-of-billions hardware category

**Finding — iiMedia Research, 2025:** China's smart-learning-machine market was estimated at approximately **RMB 27.072bn in 2024** and forecast to reach **RMB 51.441bn by 2027**. The broader education smart-hardware market was estimated at RMB 96.2bn in 2024 and RMB 112.5bn in 2025.

**Source:** iiMedia Research, *2025 China Smart Learning Machine Industry Report*.  
https://report.iimedia.cn/repo178-0/64199.html

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Caveat:** definitions differ from RUNTO's AI-learning-tablet category, so the two figures should not be combined.

---

## Market-size conclusion

The defensible conclusion is:

> **China's AI-in-education market is clearly growing, but there is no single clean market-size number.**

The most useful decomposition is:

- **GenAI-enabled education products/services:** potentially hundreds of billions RMB;
- **underlying AI technology value:** tens of billions RMB;
- **AI learning hardware:** tens of billions RMB;
- **pure AI-tutor software/app revenue:** not credibly isolated by the sources reviewed.

# 2. Regulatory Trajectory Since Double Reduction

## Finding 2.1 — Traditional K-12 academic tutoring remains structurally constrained in 2026

**Finding — Ministry of Education, March 2025:** China's national education-training supervision platform rules continue to define the regulated scope around institutions providing organized/systematic training to preschool and K-12 students and position the platform as a mechanism for continuous regulation of after-school training.

**Source:** Ministry of Education, *National After-school Education Training Supervision Platform Management Rules*, 26 Mar 2025.  
https://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A29/202503/t20250326_1184786.html

**Finding — Jiangsu Education Department, 9 July 2026:** Jiangsu continued publishing dynamic black/white lists and prohibited K-12 academic tutoring during statutory holidays, rest days and winter/summer vacations, while continuing enforcement against disguised academic tutoring.

**Source:** Jiangsu Provincial Department of Education, 9 Jul 2026.  
https://jyt.jiangsu.gov.cn/art/2026/7/9/art_58960_11801574.html

**Confidence:** High.

**Conclusion:** Double Reduction remains an active regulatory framework in 2026.

---

## Finding 2.2 — AI education is simultaneously being actively promoted

**Finding — Ministry of Education + eight ministries, 16 April 2025:** the *Opinions on Accelerating Education Digitalization* call for:

- an AI education foundation model;
- multimodal education datasets;
- AI+education application pilots;
- AI integration into curriculum, teaching and assessment;
- intelligent learning companions;
- digital mentors;
- an AI+education security framework.

**Source:** Ministry of Education, 16 Apr 2025.  
https://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A01/s7048/202504/t20250416_1187476.html

**Confidence:** High.

---

## Finding 2.3 — 2026 moved from general digitalization to a dedicated AI+Education action plan

**Finding — 8 April 2026:** China's national **AI+Education Action Plan** explicitly targets deep integration of AI across the education lifecycle and establishes a 2030 objective for a comprehensive AI-enabled education ecosystem.

**Source:** Ministry of Education and participating ministries, 8 Apr 2026.  
https://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A16/s3342/202604/t20260410_1433240.html

**Confidence:** High.

### Regulatory trajectory conclusion

China is **not simply loosening education regulation**.

It is doing something more strategically important:

> **Restricting the old business model while actively legitimizing and supporting the new technological model.**

The trajectory can be summarized as:

**Double Reduction → tighter tutoring governance → digital education → AI-enabled education → regulated AI education ecosystem.**

This is a **bifurcation**, not deregulation.

# 3. AI-Specific Regulation Affecting EdTech Products

## Finding 3.1 — Generative AI regulation applies to public-facing AI tutor services

**Finding — CAC and six ministries, effective 15 August 2023:** the *Interim Measures for the Management of Generative AI Services* apply to public-facing GenAI services in China. Providers must protect personal information, minimize unnecessary collection and take measures to prevent minors from excessive dependence/addiction.

**Source:** Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC), 13 Jul 2023; effective 15 Aug 2023.  
https://www.cac.gov.cn/2023-07/13/c_1690898327029107.htm

**Confidence:** High.

**Product implication:** an AI tutor is not merely an EdTech product; a public-facing GenAI tutor also sits within China's AI-governance regime.

---

## Finding 3.2 — GenAI filing/registration is operational

**Finding — CAC, 9 January 2026:** during 2025, China added **446 filed GenAI services** and **330 registered AI applications/functions** using already-filed models. By 31 Dec 2025 there were **748 filed GenAI services and 435 registered applications/functions**.

**Source:** CAC, 9 Jan 2026.  
https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-01/09/c_1769688009588554.htm

**Finding — CAC, 13 May 2026:** by 30 April 2026, cumulative totals had reached **868 filed GenAI services and 530 registered applications/functions**.

**Source:** CAC, 13 May 2026.  
https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-05/13/c_1780413225190669.htm

**Confidence:** High.

**Product implication:** compliance infrastructure is a normal operating requirement for Chinese public-facing AI products.

---

## Finding 3.3 — Minor data receives heightened protection

**Finding — PIPL:** information concerning children under 14 is treated as **sensitive personal information**, requiring heightened safeguards and specific consent requirements.

**Source:** CAC, *Personal Information Protection Law*, 20 Aug 2021.  
https://www.cac.gov.cn/2021-08/20/c_1631050028355286.htm

**Finding — Children's Personal Information Network Protection Regulation:** requires guardian notification/consent, purpose limitation, minimal collection, security controls and deletion mechanisms for children's online personal information.

**Source:** CAC, 23 Aug 2019.  
https://www.cac.gov.cn/2019-08/23/c_1124913903.htm

**Finding — Regulation on Protection of Minors Online:** effective 1 Jan 2024; includes additional requirements for minor-data processing and compliance audits.

**Source:** CAC, 24 Oct 2023.  
https://www.cac.gov.cn/2023-10/24/c_1699806932316206.htm

**Confidence:** High.

### Product-design implication

For an AI tutor using:

- voice;
- camera;
- facial analysis;
- emotion recognition;
- learning-behavior profiling;
- persistent conversational memory;

data minimization and parental controls become **product-architecture requirements**, not merely legal-review items.

---

## Finding 3.4 — Facial recognition involving children is specifically regulated

**Finding — CAC + Ministry of Public Security, 21 March 2025; effective 1 June 2025:** the *Measures for Security Management of Facial Recognition Technology Applications* require parental/guardian consent for processing facial information of children under 14 and impose dedicated storage/use/transfer requirements.

**Source:** CAC, 21 Mar 2025.  
https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/21/c_1744174262156096.htm

**Confidence:** High.

**Relevance:** highly relevant to camera-based digital-twin-teacher concepts.

---

## Finding 3.5 — 2026 regulation targets emotionally anthropomorphic AI

**Finding — CAC and four ministries, 10 April 2026; effective 15 July 2026:** the *Interim Measures for AI Anthropomorphic Interactive Services* regulate AI services that simulate human personality/thinking/communication. They require minor modes, parental controls and risk warnings and impose additional protections for under-14 users.

**Source:** CAC, 10 Apr 2026.  
https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-04/10/c_1777558395078289.htm

**Important caveat:** the regulation distinguishes ordinary learning/education services without continuous emotional interaction from covered anthropomorphic interactive services.

**Confidence:** High.

### Product implication

This creates a useful design distinction:

**Lower-risk direction**

> AI teacher → explains → demonstrates → listens → corrects → adapts → assesses → recommends the next learning step.

**Higher-risk direction**

> AI teacher → simulated friend/companion → emotional dependency → persistent relationship → emotional manipulation.

For a K-12 product, the first model is strategically and regulatorily cleaner.

# 4. National AI+Education Strategy and Top-Down Drivers

## Finding 4.1 — AI+Education is an explicit national strategy

**Finding — April 2025:** national policy explicitly called for AI education foundation models, AI pilots, AI-enhanced curriculum/teaching, digital mentors and human-AI collaborative teaching.

**Source:** Ministry of Education, 16 Apr 2025.  
https://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A01/s7048/202504/t20250416_1187476.html

**Finding — April 2026:** the dedicated AI+Education Action Plan established a national framework extending to 2030.

**Source:** Ministry of Education and participating ministries, 8 Apr 2026.  
https://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A16/s3342/202604/t20260410_1433240.html

**Confidence:** High.

---

## Finding 4.2 — Funding and implementation are multi-level

**Finding — April 2025:** implementation assigns roles to central and local governments; provincial education departments implement programs; development/reform and finance departments support education-digitalization projects and funding; technology departments support AI research; schools are encouraged to pilot applications.

**Source:** Ministry of Education, 16 Apr 2025.  
https://www.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A01/s7048/202504/t20250416_1187476.html

**Confidence:** High.

### Demand engines

China's AI-in-education market therefore has three overlapping demand engines:

**Government/public procurement → school/institutional demand → consumer adoption**

This differs from a purely VC-funded consumer-AI market.

---

## Finding 4.3 — School procurement is moving toward GenAI

**Finding — iResearch, February 2026:** iResearch estimates GenAI-related products/services represented approximately **25–35% of higher-education informationization procurement** and **20–30% of K-12 school procurement** in 2025.

**Source:** iResearch, Feb 2026.  
https://report.iresearch.cn/report/202602/4784.shtml

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Caveat:** these are research estimates rather than audited procurement revenue.

# 5. Investment / Funding Trends

## Finding 5.1 — Education capital overall has contracted sharply

**Finding — 22 July 2026:** China's education sector recorded **16 financing events in H1 2026**, with 12 disclosed amounts totaling approximately **RMB 121m**. Compared with H1 2025, financing-event count fell **33.3%** and disclosed financing value fell **89.6%**.

**Source:** Blackboard Insight / Sina Tech, 22 Jul 2026.  
https://tech.sina.com.cn/csj/2026-07-22/doc-iniispmy8723351.shtml

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Caveat:** the report uses disclosed financing only and applies midpoint assumptions to some non-exact deal sizes.

**Contrary evidence:** This means the broader statement "AI education is booming" should not be interpreted as "EdTech VC is booming." Overall education financing is under substantial pressure.

---

## Finding 5.2 — Capital is concentrating into selective AI-enabled niches

**Finding — July 2026:** 13 of 16 H1 2026 education financing events were angel rounds; seven projects explicitly referenced AI in their business descriptions. AI projects appeared across education technology, enterprise education services and early-childhood education.

**Source:** Blackboard Insight / Sina Tech, 22 Jul 2026.  
https://tech.sina.com.cn/csj/2026-07-22/doc-iniispmy8723351.shtml

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Interpretation:**

> Less capital overall + more selective capital + preference for AI-enabled vertical applications.

---

## Finding 5.3 — 2024 showed the same structural bifurcation

**Finding — 2024 review:** 多鲸资本 identified 81 education financing events totaling approximately RMB 25bn, with AI+education among the areas attracting early-stage capital; nine companies completed/pursued overseas listings and nine companies delisted.

**Source:** 多鲸资本, 2024 education financing review.  
https://www.djcapital.net/nd.jsp?id=1146

**Finding — June 2025:** CCID reported sharply reduced education financing since 2021 while noting capital interest in policy-aligned education technology, including several AI-education financing rounds.

**Source:** CCID, *2025 Education Industry Ten Trends*, Jun 2025.  
https://www.ccidreport.com/pub/html/market/info-zt/zt/20250617V4SD.pdf

**Confidence:** Medium.

**Caveat:** these datasets use different inclusion rules and should not be combined into one industry-wide AI-EdTech funding total.

---

## Finding 5.4 — Strategic capital remains available for AI-native education

**Finding — September 2024:** AI education provider Jingzhunxue announced **RMB 200m** strategic investment from Alibaba alongside an "ultra-anthropomorphic one-to-one AI teacher" based on Alibaba's Tongyi model.

**Source:** China Securities Journal / CLS, 20 Sep 2024.  
https://api3.cls.cn/share/article/1804799

**Confidence:** High for the disclosed transaction.

**Interpretation:** strategic technology capital can still fund AI-native education concepts even while generic EdTech financing contracts.

https://hudong.moe.gov.cn/srcsite/A01/s7048/202504/t20250416_1187476.html

**Interpretation:** internationalization is part of national education-digitalization strategy, not merely individual-company GTM activity.

---

## Finding 6.2 — China's digital education platform has global reach

**Finding — January 2026:** the Ministry of Education reported that China's National Smart Education Platform had exceeded **170m registered users** and covered **200+ countries/regions**, describing it as part of China's effort to build an internationally influential digital-education brand.

**Source:** Ministry of Education, Jan 2026.  
https://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_xwfb/xw_zt/moe_357/2026/2026_zt02/qgsdjyxw/10/202601/t20260122_1427229.html

**Caveat:** this demonstrates public digital-education internationalization, not proof that Chinese consumer AI-tutor subscriptions will automatically succeed overseas.

---

## Finding 6.3 — Southeast Asia is within the broader internationalization footprint, but there is no specific AI-tutor export subsidy identified

**Finding:** Chinese education policy supports overseas digital-education cooperation, overseas learning resources and international platforms.

**Source:** Ministry of Education, 2025–2026.  
https://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_xwfb/xw_zt/moe_357/2026/2026_zt02/qgsdjyxw/10/202601/t20260122_1427236.html

## Finding 6.4 — AI/data regulation creates export friction

**Finding:** China has continued tightening governance around AI algorithms, GenAI filing, data security and cross-border data processing while simultaneously encouraging AI development.

**Sources:** CAC, 2023–2026 GenAI and data-governance publications.  
https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-01/09/c_1769688009588554.htm

**Confidence:** High.

**Implication:** Chinese AI companies have strong AI engineering capabilities, but overseas deployment still requires localization of:

- data architecture;
- child-safety controls;
- curriculum/content;
- privacy compliance;
- AI model governance;
- local education regulation.

## 2. Do not copy the Chinese tutoring business model

**No.**

Double Reduction means the stronger Chinese opportunity is increasingly **AI as education infrastructure/tooling**, rather than recreating traditional academic tutoring through an AI avatar.

For Edupia, position the product closer to:

> **AI teacher that amplifies the learning experience**

rather than:

> **AI teacher that replaces the human teacher.**

### Better direction

### Higher-risk direction

# Decision-Grade Conclusion

**Overall assessment: strategically validated, but not directly copyable.**

The strongest evidence is the convergence of four independent signals:

1. **AI+Education is an explicit Chinese national strategy** in 2025–2026.
2. **Traditional tutoring remains constrained**, pushing innovation toward compliant AI/digital formats.
3. **Capital is selectively following AI-enabled education despite a broader collapse in EdTech financing.**
4. **China is explicitly internationalizing digital education**, while Chinese AI/K-12 companies are beginning to build overseas infrastructure.

Therefore, for Edupia's **390k/month** proposition, the key question should not be:

> "Can we copy the Chinese AI dual-teacher?"

It should be:

> **"Which components of the Chinese AI-education stack create enough measurable learning value to justify a 390k/month premium in Vietnam?"**

The most transferable components are likely:

- AI personalization;
- AI teacher;
- continuous assessment;
- learning-path adaptation;
- high-quality local ESL content;
- parent/teacher visibility;
- human-AI collaborative learning.

---

# Gaps and Unanswered Questions

1. **Pure B2C AI-tutor subscription TAM in China:** no sufficiently credible current named source found that isolates consumer AI-tutor subscription revenue.
2. **AI dual-teacher/digital-twin-teacher market size:** no sufficiently credible independent market-size figure found.
3. **AI-learning hardware by device type:** current sources disagree because definitions differ between AI learning tablets, smart learning machines and broader education smart hardware.
4. **Total China AI-EdTech VC/PE investment in 2025–2026:** credible sources provide education-sector totals and individual AI rounds, but not a transparent comprehensive AI-only dataset.
5. **Government subsidy specifically for consumer AI tutors:** strong public-sector AI+Education support exists, but no nationwide consumer AI-tutor subsidy was identified.
6. **Specific Chinese government policy encouraging AI-tutor exports to Vietnam/ASEAN:** strong digital-education internationalization policy exists, but no China-wide AI-tutor export subsidy was identified.
7. **Voice-cloning consent specifically for AI teachers:** broader biometric/minor-data rules create constraints, but no current official rule was found that specifically defines consent requirements for cloning a teacher's voice.
