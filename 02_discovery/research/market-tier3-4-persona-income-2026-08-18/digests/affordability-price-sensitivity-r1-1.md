- claim: AFFORDABILITY CALCULATION (derived, not sourced directly). Using rural per-capita monthly income of 4,504,500 VND (GSO 2024) and household size of 3.6 persons/household in rural areas (GSO 2024), estimated rural household income ≈ 16,216,200 VND/month. The 390,000-400,000 VND/month price represents: (a) 8.66%-8.88% of PER-CAPITA rural income, and (b) 2.40%-2.47% of estimated rural HOUSEHOLD income (assuming one subscription serves the whole household, e.g. one child). As a per-capita share, 390-400k sits close to typical "affordable/borderline" thresholds used in Vietnamese consumer-finance guidance (life-insurance premium guidance elsewhere in this digest suggests 10-15% of income as a normal recurring-spend ceiling); as a household share it looks clearly affordable on paper, though this ignores competing household expenses (school fees, food, other subscriptions) and that income is highly skewed regionally (see next claim).

## Leads for follow-up

- The State Bank of Vietnam's original survey (41% rural payment-literacy gap) should be traced to its primary publication — only found via a secondary aggregator (Antom) this round.
- No tier-3/4-specific or rural-specific pricing/user-count was found for ANY Vietnamese ed-tech product (ELSA, Kyna, VUIHOC, Manabie). All traction figures found are national or global aggregates. This is the single biggest evidence gap for question 3.
- Manabie's tier 2/3 city strategy, pricing, and OMO (online-merge-offline) rural/provincial footprint could not be found via search this round — worth a direct fetch of Manabie's investor materials or Tech in Asia / e27 coverage next round.
- Q&Me and Decision Lab both publish relevant Vietnam consumer research (Q&Me "Vietnam 2025 Trends," Decision Lab "Connected Consumer Q2 2025") but their landing pages are gated behind lead-capture forms; the actual report content (which likely contains province-tier price-sensitivity data) was not accessible via direct fetch. Worth trying Decision Lab's public blog posts/press summaries specifically, or PwC's "Voice of the Consumer Survey 2025 - Vietnam Snapshot," which was found but not yet fetched.
- Regional income breakdown (Southeast ~7.1M vs. Northern Midlands & Mountains ~3.8M per capita/month) suggests the "tier 3/4" persona is NOT homogeneous — a single national affordability number likely masks a >80% income gap between richer provincial towns (e.g. near Southeast/Red River Delta) and poorer mountainous provinces. Worth building two sub-personas (affluent tier-3 vs. poor tier-4) rather than one.
- No Van Westendorp or explicit price-elasticity study specific to rural/provincial Vietnam ed-tech or subscription services was found. This remains an open gap (see below).
